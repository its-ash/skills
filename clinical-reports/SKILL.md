# Clinical Report Writing

Clinical report writing documents medical information with precision, accuracy, and regulatory compliance. This skill covers four categories: case reports for journal publication, diagnostic reports for clinical practice, clinical trial reports for regulatory submission, and patient documentation for medical records. Apply for healthcare documentation, research dissemination, and regulatory compliance.

**Critical Principle:** Clinical reports must be accurate, complete, objective, and compliant with applicable regulations (HIPAA, FDA, ICH-GCP). Patient privacy and data integrity are paramount.

## When to Use This Skill

- Writing clinical case reports for journal submission (CARE guidelines)
- Creating diagnostic reports (radiology, pathology, laboratory)
- Documenting clinical trial data and adverse events
- Preparing clinical study reports (CSR) for regulatory submission
- Writing patient progress notes, SOAP notes, and clinical summaries
- Drafting discharge summaries, H&P documents, or consultation notes
- Ensuring HIPAA compliance and proper de-identification
- Validating clinical documentation for completeness and accuracy
- Preparing serious adverse event (SAE) reports
- Creating data safety monitoring board (DSMB) reports

## 1. Clinical Case Reports (CARE Guidelines)

Case reports describe unusual presentations, novel diagnoses, or rare complications. Follow the CARE checklist:

### Title
- Include "case report" or "case study"
- Indicate area of focus
- Example: "Unusual Presentation of Acute Myocardial Infarction in a Young Patient: A Case Report"

### Keywords
- 2-5 keywords for indexing
- Use MeSH terms when possible

### Abstract (150-250 words, structured)
- Introduction: What is unique?
- Patient concerns: Primary symptoms and key history
- Diagnoses: Primary and secondary
- Interventions: Key treatments
- Outcomes: Clinical outcome and follow-up
- Conclusions: Main takeaway

### Introduction
- Brief background on condition
- Why this case is novel
- Brief literature review of similar cases

### Patient Information
- Demographics (age, sex, race/ethnicity if relevant)
- Medical, family, social history
- Relevant comorbidities
- **De-identify**: Remove or alter 18 HIPAA identifiers
- **Document consent**: Informed consent for publication

### Clinical Findings
- Chief complaint and presenting symptoms
- Physical examination findings
- Timeline of symptoms (use table or figure)

### Timeline
- Chronological summary of key events
- Dates of symptoms, diagnosis, interventions, outcomes
- Format as table or figure

### Diagnostic Assessment
- Tests performed (labs, imaging, procedures)
- Results and interpretation
- Differential diagnosis considered
- Rationale for final diagnosis

### Therapeutic Interventions
- Medications (names, dosages, routes, duration)
- Procedures or surgeries
- Non-pharmacological interventions
- Reasoning for choices

### Follow-up and Outcomes
- Clinical outcome (resolution, improvement, unchanged, worsened)
- Follow-up duration and frequency
- Long-term outcomes
- Patient-reported outcomes
- Adherence

### Discussion
- Strengths and novelty
- Comparison to literature
- Limitations
- Potential mechanisms
- Clinical implications
- Unanswered questions

### Patient Perspective (optional)
- Patient experience and viewpoint
- Quality of life impact
- Patient-reported outcomes

### Informed Consent Statement
- Document patient consent for publication
- If deceased or unable: describe proxy consent
- Pediatric: parental/guardian consent
- Example: "Written informed consent was obtained from the patient for publication of this case report and accompanying images. A copy is available for review by the Editor-in-Chief."

### Journal-Specific Requirements
- Word count limits (typically 1500-3000)
- Figure/table limits
- Reference style (AMA, Vancouver, APA)
- Structured vs. unstructured abstract
- Check journal instructions before submission

### De-identification (18 HIPAA Identifiers)
Remove or alter:
1. Names
2. Geographic subdivisions smaller than state
3. Dates (except year)
4. Telephone numbers
5. Fax numbers
6. Email addresses
7. Social Security numbers
8. Medical record numbers
9. Health plan beneficiary numbers
10. Account numbers
11. Certificate/license numbers
12. Vehicle identifiers
13. Device identifiers
14. Web URLs
15. IP addresses
16. Biometric identifiers
17. Full-face photographs
18. Any other unique identifying characteristic

**Best Practices:**
- Use "the patient" instead of names
- Report age ranges ("woman in her 60s") or exact age if relevant
- Use approximate dates or intervals ("3 months prior")
- Remove institution names unless necessary
- Blur or crop identifying features in images
- Obtain explicit consent for potentially identifying information

## 2. Diagnostic Reports

### Radiology Reports

**Standard Structure:**

1. **Patient Demographics**: Name/ID, DOB/age, MRN, exam date/time
2. **Clinical Indication**: Reason, relevant history, specific clinical question
   - Example: "Rule out pulmonary embolism in patient with acute dyspnea"
3. **Technique**: Modality, anatomical region, contrast (type, route, volume), protocol, technical quality/limitations
   - Example: "Contrast-enhanced CT chest/abdomen/pelvis with 100 mL IV iodinated contrast. No oral contrast."
4. **Comparison**: Prior studies available, dates, stability/change
5. **Findings**: Systematic, organ-by-organ, positive findings first then pertinent negatives, measurements, standardized terminology (ACR lexicon, RadLex)
6. **Impression/Conclusion**: Concise summary, answers clinical question, differential if applicable, recommendations, level of certainty

**Structured Reporting Templates:**
- Lung nodules: Lung-RADS
- Breast: BI-RADS
- Liver: LI-RADS
- Prostate: PI-RADS
- CT colonography: C-RADS

### Pathology Reports

**Surgical Pathology Structure:**

1. **Patient Information**: Name, identifiers, DOB, age, sex, ordering physician, MRN, specimen received date
2. **Specimen Information**: Type (biopsy, excision, resection), anatomical site, laterality, number of specimens/blocks/slides
3. **Clinical History**: Relevant clinical info, indication, prior diagnoses
4. **Gross Description**: Macroscopic appearance, size, weight, color, consistency, orientation markers, sectioning approach
5. **Microscopic Description**: Histological findings, cellular characteristics, architectural patterns, malignancy presence, margins, special stains/IHC
6. **Diagnosis**: Primary diagnosis, grade/stage (cancer), margin status, lymph node status, synoptic reporting (CAP protocols)
7. **Comment**: Additional context, differential, recommendations

**Synoptic Cancer Reporting (CAP):**
- Tumor site, size, histologic type, grade
- Extent of invasion, lymphovascular invasion, perineural invasion
- Margins, lymph nodes (examined/positive)
- Pathologic stage (TNM)
- Ancillary studies (molecular markers, biomarkers)

### Laboratory Reports

**Standard Components:**

1. **Patient/Specimen Info**: Identifiers, specimen type, collection date/time, received date/time, ordering provider
2. **Test Name and Method**: Full name, methodology (immunoassay, PCR, etc.), accession number
3. **Results**: Quantitative/qualitative result, units, reference range, flags (H/L), critical values highlighted
   - Example: "Hemoglobin: 8.5 g/dL (L) [Ref: 12.0-16.0]"
4. **Interpretation**: Clinical significance, suggested follow-up, correlation, therapeutic ranges
5. **Quality Control**: Specimen adequacy, quality issues (hemolyzed, lipemic, clotted), processing delays, technical limitations

**Critical Value Reporting:**
- Life-threatening results require immediate notification
- Examples: glucose <40 or >500 mg/dL, potassium <2.5 or >6.5 mEq/L
- Document notification time and recipient

## 3. Clinical Trial Reports

### Serious Adverse Event (SAE) Reports

**Definition:** An adverse event is serious if it:
- Results in death
- Is life-threatening
- Requires inpatient hospitalization or prolongation
- Results in persistent/significant disability/incapacity
- Is a congenital anomaly/birth defect
- Requires intervention to prevent permanent impairment

**SAE Report Components:**

1. **Study Information**: Protocol number/title, phase, sponsor, PI, IND/IDE, NCT number
2. **Patient Information (De-identified)**: Subject ID, age, sex, race/ethnicity, study arm, consent date, first intervention date
3. **Event Information**: Narrative description, onset date, resolution date (or ongoing), severity (mild/moderate/severe), seriousness criteria, outcome
4. **Causality Assessment**: Relationship to intervention (unrelated/unlikely/possible/probable/definite), to procedures, to underlying disease, rationale
5. **Action Taken**: Intervention modification, concomitant medications, hospitalization details, outcome/follow-up plan
6. **Expectedness**: Expected per protocol/brochure, unexpected requiring expedited reporting, comparison to known safety profile
7. **Narrative**: Detailed description, timeline, clinical course, lab/diagnostic results, final diagnosis
8. **Reporter Information**: Name, contact, report date, signature

**Regulatory Timelines:**
- Fatal/life-threatening unexpected SAEs: 7 days preliminary, 15 days complete
- Other serious unexpected: 15 days
- IRB notification: per institutional policy (typically 5-10 days)

### Clinical Study Reports (CSR) - ICH-E3 Structure

1. **Title Page**: Study title/protocol number, sponsor/investigator, report date/version
2. **Synopsis** (5-15 pages): Standalone summary of objectives, methods, results, conclusions, key efficacy/safety findings
3. **Table of Contents**
4. **Abbreviations and Definitions**
5. **Ethics** (Section 2): IRB/IEC approvals, informed consent process, GCP compliance
6. **Investigators and Study Structure** (Section 3): Investigators/sites, organization, monitoring/QA
7. **Introduction** (Section 4): Background, rationale, objectives
8. **Study Objectives and Plan** (Section 5): Design, primary/secondary objectives, endpoints, sample size
9. **Study Patients** (Section 6): Inclusion/exclusion criteria, disposition, protocol deviations, demographics/baseline
10. **Efficacy Evaluation** (Section 7): Analysis sets (ITT, PP, safety), baseline characteristics, primary/secondary endpoint results, subgroup analyses, dropouts/missing data
11. **Safety Evaluation** (Section 8): Exposure extent, AE summary tables, SAE narratives, lab values, vital signs, deaths
12. **Discussion and Conclusions** (Section 9): Interpretation, benefit-risk, clinical implications
13. **Tables, Figures, Graphs** (Section 10)
14. **References** (Section 11)
15. **Appendices** (Section 12): Protocol/amendments, sample CRFs, investigator/EC lists, consent forms, investigator's brochure references, publications

**Key Principles:** Objectivity, transparency, comprehensive data, adherence to statistical analysis plan, clear safety presentation, integrated appendices.

### Protocol Deviations

**Categories:**
- Minor: No significant impact on safety or data integrity
- Major: May impact safety, data integrity, or conduct
- Violation: Serious deviation requiring immediate action and reporting

**Documentation:** Description, date, subject ID, impact on safety/data, corrective/preventive actions (CAPA), root cause analysis, preventive measures.

## 4. Patient Clinical Documentation

### SOAP Notes

**S - Subjective**
- Patient-reported symptoms and concerns
- History of present illness (HPI)
- Relevant review of systems (ROS)
- Patient's own words (use quotes)
- Example: "Patient reports worsening shortness of breath over 3 days, particularly with exertion. Denies chest pain, fever, or cough."

**O - Objective**
- Measurable findings
- Vital signs (T, BP, HR, RR, SpO2)
- Physical exam (organized by system)
- Lab/imaging results
- Example:
  - Vitals: T 98.6°F, BP 142/88, HR 92, RR 22, SpO2 91% RA
  - General: Mild respiratory distress
  - CV: Regular rhythm, no murmurs
  - Pulm: Bilateral crackles at bases
  - Ext: 2+ pitting edema bilateral

**A - Assessment**
- Clinical impression/diagnosis
- Differential diagnosis
- Severity and stability
- Progress toward goals
- Example:
  1. Acute decompensated heart failure, NYHA Class III
  2. Hypertension, poorly controlled
  3. CKD stage 3

**P - Plan**
- Diagnostic plan (further testing)
- Therapeutic plan (medications, procedures)
- Patient education/counseling
- Follow-up arrangements
- Example:
  - Diagnostics: BNP, CXR, echo
  - Therapeutics: Increase furosemide 40 mg PO BID, continue lisinopril 10 mg daily, fluid restriction 1.5 L/day
  - Education: Signs of worsening HF, daily weights
  - Follow-up: Cardiology in 1 week, call if weight gain >2 lbs/day

**Documentation Tips:**
- Be concise but complete
- Use standard medical abbreviations
- Document time of encounter
- Sign and date all notes
- Avoid speculation or judgment
- Document medical necessity for billing
- Include patient response to treatment

### History and Physical (H&P)

**Components:**

1. **Chief Complaint (CC)**: Brief statement in patient's words
   - Example: "Chest pain for 2 hours"
2. **History of Present Illness (HPI)**: Chronological narrative, use OPQRST for pain:
   - Onset: When started?
   - Provocation/Palliation: Better/worse?
   - Quality: What does it feel like?
   - Region/Radiation: Where? Spread?
   - Severity: 0-10 scale
   - Timing: Constant/intermittent? Duration?
   - Associated symptoms, prior evaluations/treatments
3. **Past Medical History (PMH)**: Chronic conditions, hospitalizations, surgeries
4. **Medications**: Current with doses/frequencies, OTC, herbals, allergies/reactions
5. **Allergies**: Drug (with reaction type), food, environmental
6. **Family History (FH)**: First-degree relatives' conditions, age/cause of death, hereditary conditions
7. **Social History (SH)**: Tobacco (pack-years), alcohol (drinks/week), illicit drugs, occupation, living situation, sexual history if relevant
8. **Review of Systems (ROS)**: Systematic by organ system (10-14 systems), pertinent positives/negatives
   - Systems: Constitutional, Eyes, ENT, CV, Respiratory, GI, GU, Musculoskeletal, Skin, Neuro, Psych, Endocrine, Heme/Lymph

### Discharge Summary

**Required Elements:**
- Admission and discharge dates
- Admitting and discharge diagnoses
- Principal procedure(s)
- Hospital course: Narrative of key events, consultations, procedures, complications, response to treatment
- Discharge condition
- Discharge medications (reconciled with admission meds)
- Discharge instructions: Activity, diet, wound care, medications, warning signs
- Follow-up appointments
- Pending test results
- Attending physician signature

## Validation Checklist

Before finalizing any clinical report:

- [ ] All patient identifiers removed or altered per HIPAA (18 identifiers)
- [ ] Informed consent documented for case reports
- [ ] At least one schematic/figure generated via scientific-schematics skill
- [ ] Terminology follows standard lexicons (ACR, RadLex, CAP, MeSH)
- [ ] Measurements include units and reference ranges
- [ ] Abbreviations defined at first use
- [ ] Dates use relative intervals or year-only for de-identified reports
- [ ] Regulatory timelines met for SAE/CSR submissions
- [ ] Narrative sections are objective and free of speculation
- [ ] All required sections present per applicable guideline (CARE, ICH-E3, CAP, etc.)
- [ ] Report reviewed for completeness by second clinician when possible
- [ ] Version control and date stamps applied
- [ ] Distribution list documented for regulatory reports

## Common Pitfalls

- Including identifiable information in case reports
- Omitting consent documentation
- Using non-standard abbreviations without definition
- Missing pertinent negatives in diagnostic reports
- Failing to document causality assessment for SAEs
- Inconsistent terminology across report sections
- Omitting timeline/figure for case reports
- Not reconciling medications in discharge summaries
- Submitting CSR without complete appendices
- Using subjective language in objective sections
