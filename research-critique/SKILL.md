# Research Critique and Scientific Peer Review

This skill provides a systematic framework for evaluating scientific rigor, methodology, statistical validity, and the integrity of research claims. It is used to conduct formal peer reviews, assess grant proposals, and critically analyze scientific literature.

## When to Use

- Conducting peer review for journals or grant applications.
- Evaluating research methodology, experimental design, and statistical validity.
- Identifying biases, confounding variables, and logical fallacies in scientific claims.
- Assessing evidence quality using GRADE or Cochrane Risk of Bias (ROB) frameworks.
- Reviewing compliance with reporting standards (e.g., CONSORT, PRISMA, STROBE, ARRIVE).
- Providing constructive feedback on scientific writing and data presentation.

## Peer Review Workflow

### Stage 1: Initial Assessment
Determine the manuscript's scope, novelty, and overall quality.
- Identify the central research question or hypothesis.
- Evaluate if the work is scientifically sound and significant for the intended venue.
- Identify any immediate major flaws that preclude publication.

### Stage 2: Section-by-Section Evaluation

#### Abstract and Title
- Verify that the title is specific and the abstract accurately reflects the content and conclusions.
- Ensure key findings and methods are summarized for a broad scientific audience.

#### Introduction
- Assess if the background is current and the research question is clearly motivated.
- Verify that the work's originality and significance are articulated and relevant literature is cited.

#### Methods
- **Reproducibility:** Can the study be replicated from the provided description?
- **Rigor:** Are protocols, reagents, equipment, and parameters sufficiently detailed?
- **Ethics:** Are IRB/IACUC approvals, informed consent, and the 3Rs (Replacement, Reduction, Refinement) documented?
- **Design:** Evaluate randomization, blinding, inclusion/exclusion criteria, and control groups.

#### Results
- **Presentation:** Are results logical? Are figures/tables clear and properly labeled?
- **Statistics:** Are effect sizes, confidence intervals, and p-values reported correctly?
- **Objectivity:** Are results presented without over-interpretation or selective reporting?

#### Discussion
- **Interpretation:** Are conclusions supported by the data? Distinguish speculation from data-driven results.
- **Limitations:** Are study limitations acknowledged and discussed?
- **Context:** Are findings placed within existing literature without ignoring contradictory evidence?

#### References
- Check for completeness, currency, and balance (including contrary viewpoints).
- Identify excessive or inappropriate self-citation.

### Stage 3: Methodological and Statistical Rigor

#### Statistical Assessment
- **Assumptions:** Verify normality, independence, and homoscedasticity.
- **Power:** Check for a priori power analysis and adequate sample size.
- **Multiple Testing:** Ensure correction (e.g., Bonferroni, FDR) is applied for multiple hypotheses.
- **Interpretation:** Ensure statistical significance is not conflated with practical importance.

#### Experimental Design
- Evaluate the choice of design (Experimental, Quasi-experimental, or Observational).
- Check for internal validity (causal inference) and external validity (generalizability).
- Assess construct validity: Do measures capture the intended constructs?

### Stage 4: Reproducibility and Transparency
- **Data Availability:** Check for raw data deposits in public repositories and provided accession numbers.
- **Code:** Verify if analysis code is available (e.g., GitHub, Zenodo).
- **Standards:** Ensure adherence to discipline-specific guidelines (CONSORT, PRISMA, etc.).

### Stage 5: Figure and Data Integrity
- **Quality:** Check for high resolution, clear axis labels with units, and colorblind-friendly schemes.
- **Integrity:** Inspect for signs of image manipulation (splicing, duplications) in blots or gels.
- **Clarity:** Ensure figures can stand alone with their legends.

## Bias and Fallacy Detection

### Systematic Bias Review
- **Researcher Bias:** Look for confirmation bias, HARKing (Hypothesizing After Results are Known), and cherry-picking.
- **Selection Bias:** Identify sampling bias, volunteer bias, and differential attrition.
- **Measurement Bias:** Check for observer bias, recall bias, and social desirability.
- **Analysis Bias:** Detect p-hacking, outcome switching, and subgroup fishing.
- **Confounding:** Identify variables that affect both exposure and outcome that were not controlled.

### Logical Fallacies in Science
- **Causation:** Post hoc ergo propter hoc, confusing correlation with causation, or reverse causation.
- **Generalization:** Hasty generalizations from small samples or ecological fallacies.
- **Statistical:** Base rate neglect, Texas sharpshooter fallacy, or the Prosecutor's fallacy.
- **Structural:** False dichotomies, moving goalposts, or unfalsifiable claims.

## Evidence Quality Assessment

### Evidence Hierarchy
1. Systematic Reviews/Meta-analyses (Highest for intervention)
2. Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs)
3. Cohort Studies
4. Case-Control Studies
5. Cross-sectional Studies
6. Case Series/Reports
7. Expert Opinion (Lowest)

### GRADE Framework Application
- **Downgrade for:** Risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, or publication bias.
- **Upgrade for:** Large effect sizes or dose-response relationships.

## Structuring the Review Report

### 1. Summary Statement
- Brief synopsis of the research.
- Overall recommendation (Accept, Minor Revision, Major Revision, Reject).
- Bulleted key strengths and key weaknesses.

### 2. Major Comments
Number these sequentially. Focus on fundamental flaws:
- Methodological failures or inappropriate statistics.
- Unsupported conclusions or missing critical controls.
- Serious ethical or reproducibility concerns.
- *Requirement:* State the issue, explain why it is problematic, and suggest a specific solution.

### 3. Minor Comments
Number these sequentially. Focus on improvements:
- Unclear labels, typos, or missing methodological details.
- Suggestions for better data presentation.

### 4. Questions for Authors
- Specific requests for clarification on contradictory results or missing information.

## Special Case: Presentation Review

When reviewing slide decks (PDF/PPT), focus on visual communication and cognitive load.

### Visual and Layout Criteria
- **Readability:** Minimum 18pt font; high contrast (4.5:1 minimum).
- **Layout:** No text overflow, element overlaps, or truncated bullets.
- **Density:** One main idea per slide; 3-6 concise bullets maximum.
- **Visuals:** Simplified figures (not copy-pasted from papers) with readable labels.

### Content and Flow
- **Structure:** Clear arc (Intro $\rightarrow$ Methods $\rightarrow$ Results $\rightarrow$ Discussion).
- **Balance:** Results/Data should dominate 40-50% of the content.
- **Timing:** Approximately one slide per minute of talk duration.

## Checklist for Final Review
- [ ] Summary statement conveys a clear overall assessment.
- [ ] Major concerns are justified and actionable.
- [ ] Statistical methods and power are evaluated.
- [ ] Reproducibility, data availability, and ethics are verified.
- [ ] Figure integrity and quality are checked.
- [ ] Tone is constructive, professional, and objective.
- [ ] Recommendation is consistent with the identified issues.
