# Launch Strategy

Help users design a launch that creates early user access, gathers feedback, and builds sustained momentum across multiple channels.

## When to use
- You are releasing a new product, major feature, or public update.
- You need a roadmap from internal testing to full public availability.
- You want to coordinate email, blog, social, and partner outreach.
- You are considering a Product Hunt or similar marketplace launch.

---

## Launch Framework

### Owned Channels
- Use channels you control: email list, blog, podcast, community (Slack/Discord), website, in‑app messaging.
- Start with 1–2 that match your audience.
- Publish regular content that drives users back to your product.
- Track engagement to improve over time.

### Rented Channels
- Platforms you do not control: X/Twitter, LinkedIn, Instagram, YouTube, Reddit, app stores, marketplaces.
- Choose 1–2 where your target users are active.
- Create posts that funnel traffic to owned channels (e.g., link to newsletter signup).
- Do not rely on these channels as the sole source of users.

### Borrowed Channels
- Leverage third‑party audiences: guest blog posts, podcast interviews, newsletter features, co‑hosted webinars, influencer reviews, affiliate programs.
- Identify industry leaders with overlapping audiences.
- Pitch win‑win collaborations and provide clear value.
- Convert borrowed attention into owned relationships (capture email, invite to community).

## Five‑Phase Launch Process

| Phase | Goal | Core Actions |
|-------|------|--------------|
| **1. Internal Launch** | Validate core functionality with trusted users. | Recruit a handful of internal testers. Collect usability feedback. Ensure a demo‑ready prototype exists. |
| **2. Alpha Launch** | Obtain first external validation and start building a waitlist. | Build a simple landing page with an early‑access signup form. Invite users individually. Deploy a production‑ready MVP (even if limited). |
| **3. Beta Launch** | Scale testing and generate buzz. | Open the waitlist to more users (free or paid). Release teaser content about the problem you solve. Invite friends, investors, and micro‑influencers to test and share. Add visible “Beta” labels in the UI. |
| **4. Early‑Access Launch** | Validate at scale and prepare for full release. | Publish screenshots, GIFs, and demo videos. Run user research with incentives (credits, swag). Optionally run a product‑market‑fit survey. Release in controlled batches (5‑10 % of users) or all at once under an “early‑access” banner. |
| **5. Full Launch** | Capture maximum visibility and convert to paying users. | Open self‑serve sign‑ups. Activate pricing if not already live. Announce across all channels (email, blog, in‑app pop‑ups, website banner, social, Product Hunt, Hacker News, etc.). |

### Phase‑by‑Phase Checklist
- **Internal:** schedule 1‑on‑1 demo calls, record feedback notes.
- **Alpha:** create landing page, set up email capture, send personalized invites.
- **Beta:** prepare teaser copy, design beta badge, schedule social teasers.
- **Early‑Access:** produce demo video, draft survey, set batch‑release schedule.
- **Full:** finalize pricing page, prepare launch assets (screenshots, GIFs, video), schedule all‑channel announcements.

## Product Hunt Tactics
1. **Pre‑launch preparation**
   - Build relationships with active hunters, moderators, and niche newsletters.
   - Craft a concise tagline, high‑quality hero image, and a 30‑second demo video.
   - Prepare a landing page that captures email addresses from Product Hunt traffic.
2. **Launch day**
   - Treat the day as an all‑day event. Post the listing early (00:00 UTC) and stay online.
   - Respond to every comment within minutes. Answer questions, thank commenters, and add clarifications.
   - Encourage your existing audience to up‑vote and comment.
3. **Post‑launch**
   - Export the list of commenters and send a thank‑you email with a follow‑up offer.
   - Move traffic to owned channels (email list, community) for continued nurturing.
   - Publish a post‑launch blog recap to sustain momentum.

## Post‑Launch Marketing
- **Onboarding sequence**: automate a series of emails that introduce key features, use cases, and quick‑start guides.
- **Round‑up communication**: include the launch announcement in your regular newsletter for users who missed the live announcement.
- **Competitive positioning**: create a comparison page that highlights why your solution is superior.
- **Interactive demo**: build a no‑code demo (e.g., Navattic) and embed it on the product page.
- **Analytics**: track sign‑ups, activation rates, and churn for the first 30 days. Use data to iterate on messaging.

---

## Ongoing Announcement Planning
1. **Prioritise announcements** using a three‑tier matrix:
   - **Major** (new product, major feature set): full multi‑channel campaign.
   - **Medium** (new integration, UI refresh): targeted email + in‑app banner.
   - **Minor** (bug fix, small tweak): changelog entry only.
2. **Space out releases** to avoid audience fatigue. Schedule announcements 2–4 weeks apart when possible.
3. **Reuse high‑performing assets** (copy, visuals, video style) that previously drove engagement.
4. **Signal continuous development** by publishing regular changelogs and roadmap updates.

---

## Common Pitfalls & Mitigations
- **Relying solely on rented channels** – diversify with owned and borrowed channels to protect against algorithm changes.
- **Launching without a clear waitlist or capture mechanism** – always include an email capture form before traffic spikes.
- **Neglecting post‑launch onboarding** – users who sign up but never see the product will churn; automate onboarding.
- **Over‑communicating minor updates** – keep minor changes to a simple changelog to avoid noise.
- **Missing real‑time engagement on launch day** – assign a dedicated team member to monitor comments and support tickets.

## Launch Checklist
### Pre‑Launch
- Build a landing page with clear value proposition and email capture.
- Assemble an early‑access list (internal, alpha, beta).
- Set up owned channels (email list, blog, community).
- Optimize rented channel profiles (Twitter, LinkedIn, YouTube).
- Identify borrowed channel partners (podcasts, newsletters, influencers).
- Prepare Product Hunt assets (tagline, visuals, demo video) if applicable.
- Create launch assets: screenshots, GIFs, demo video, press kit.
- Finalise onboarding flow and in‑app messaging.
- Implement analytics and conversion tracking.

### Launch Day
- Send announcement email to list.
- Publish blog post and website banner.
- Post scheduled social updates.
- Activate Product Hunt listing (if used).
- Show in‑app announcement for existing users.
- Keep team on standby for real‑time comment response and issue monitoring.

### Post‑Launch (0‑30 days)
- Run automated onboarding email sequence.
- Follow up with engaged prospects from comments and sign‑ups.
- Include launch recap in regular newsletter.
- Publish comparison and feature‑highlight pages.
- Release interactive demo for visitors.
- Collect feedback, prioritize bugs, and plan the next announcement.

## Questions to Clarify Your Situation
- What type of launch are you planning (new product, major feature, minor update)?
- How large is your current audience and which owned channels are active?
- What is your target launch timeline?
- Have you launched before? What worked and what didn’t?
- Are you planning to use Product Hunt or a similar marketplace?
