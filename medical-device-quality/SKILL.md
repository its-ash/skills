# Medical Device Quality

Manage medical device quality documentation and risk management across the product lifecycle. This skill covers document control, change control, regulatory documentation, electronic records, and ISO 14971 risk management.

## When to use

- Implement or audit document control under ISO 13485.
- Prepare, review, or maintain regulatory technical documentation.
- Manage document versions, approvals, distribution, retention, and disposal.
- Implement or validate an electronic document management system.
- Manage document changes and configuration control.
- Plan and perform ISO 14971 risk management activities.
- Maintain the risk management file and update it after design or post-market changes.
- Coordinate quality documentation and risk outputs with QMS, CAPA, audits, and submissions.

## Document control system

- Define a document taxonomy, numbering scheme, version rules, and lifecycle statuses.
- Use standardized templates for document creation.
- Assign authors, reviewers, approvers, and quality assurance roles.
- Control distribution, access permissions, and external document coordination.
- Schedule periodic reviews and manage obsolete documents.
- Define retention periods, archiving, legal hold, and secure disposal.
- Approve documents for regulatory submission or internal use only after review.

## Regulatory documentation oversight

- Manage multi-jurisdictional requirements, including EU MDR Annex II and III, FDA 510(k), PMA, and De Novo, ISO 13485, ISO 14971, Health Canada, and TGA.
- Verify technical accuracy, regulatory compliance, format, structure, cross-references, and traceability.
- Assemble submission packages and coordinate authority queries and responses.
- Update documentation after submission amendments, variations, and market access changes.

## Change control and configuration management

1. Initiate the change request with justification, impact assessment, stakeholder notification, and documentation.
2. Review and approve the change through technical review, regulatory impact assessment, risk assessment, resource evaluation, and authorization.
3. Implement the change through document revision, training, communication, system update, deployment, and verification.
4. Verify and close the change through implementation verification, effectiveness assessment, stakeholder confirmation, and record completion.
5. Monitor after closure through performance monitoring, issue resolution, lessons learned, and process improvement.

## Document management system implementation

- Define functional requirements and regulatory compliance requirements.
- Evaluate and select the system and implementation approach.
- Configure storage, workflow management, system integration, and user interface.
- Validate the system before deployment.
- Train users and verify competency.
- Use phased rollout and monitor performance after deployment.

## Electronic records and 21 CFR Part 11

- Validate electronic signature and record systems.
- Manage user authentication, authorization, and authority checks.
- Maintain audit trails, access controls, and operational controls.
- Protect electronic record integrity and authenticity.
- Manage retention, archiving, system migration, and legacy data.
- Maintain inspection readiness.

## Multi-language documentation

- Coordinate professional translation.
- Verify medical and technical terminology accuracy.
- Adapt content for local market requirements.
- Synchronize versions across languages.

## Documentation quality and audit

- Track accuracy, compliance rate, cycle time, approval duration, user satisfaction, and system performance.
- Audit document control procedures, system functionality, user competency, and regulatory compliance.
- Review document accuracy, completeness, guideline adherence, traceability, version control, and change management.
- Improve workflows, technology, user experience, and regulatory alignment.

## ISO 14971 risk management process

- Plan risk management with objectives, scope, acceptability criteria, team roles, and a risk management file.
- Analyze intended use, reasonably foreseeable misuse, hazards, hazardous situations, and risk estimates.
- Evaluate risk acceptability, benefit-risk, and the need for risk control.
- Implement risk controls and evaluate residual risk.
- Collect and analyze production and post-production information.
- Update the risk management file and review risk controls throughout the lifecycle.

## Risk analysis

1. Define intended use and context, including indication, patient population, use environment, user characteristics, and training.
2. Identify hazards for hardware, software, combination products, and connected devices.
3. Analyze hazardous situations, including event sequences, foreseeable misuse, use errors, single faults, and multiple faults.

Hardware hazards include mechanical, electrical, thermal, and chemical hazards. Software hazards follow IEC 62304. Combination products require drug-device interaction analysis. Connected devices require cybersecurity and data privacy analysis.

## Risk estimation and evaluation

- Estimate probability using statistical data, literature, and expert judgment.
- Estimate severity using clinical outcome evaluation and classification.
- Determine risk level using a risk matrix.
- Evaluate acceptability against defined criteria and document justification.
- If risk is acceptable, document it.
- If risk is not acceptable, implement risk control.
- Evaluate residual risk and perform benefit-risk analysis before the final acceptability decision.

## Risk control

Follow the risk control hierarchy:

1. Inherent safety by design: eliminate hazards, use fail-safe design, apply redundancy and diversity, and integrate human factors engineering.
2. Protective measures in the device: alarms, automatic shut-off, physical barriers, shields, and software safety functions.
3. Information for safety: training, labeling, instructions for use, warnings, contraindications, and precautions.

Verify risk control effectiveness through testing, validation protocols, result analysis, and performance monitoring.

## Advanced risk management

- Software: classify software safety as Class A, B, or C; analyze software contribution to hazards; implement architecture and design safety measures; integrate with the risk management file.
- Cybersecurity: model threats, identify assets and vulnerabilities, analyze threat sources and attack vectors, assess impact, estimate and prioritize risk, and implement preventive, detective, corrective, and compensating controls.
- Human factors: analyze tasks and use scenarios, identify critical tasks and use errors, estimate probability and severity, and apply design controls and user interface optimization.

## Risk management file

Maintain these records:

- Risk management plan.
- Risk analysis records.
- Risk control records.
- Production and post-production information.
- Risk management report.

Update the file for design changes, post-market information, risk control effectiveness reviews, and periodic risk management reviews.

## Cross-functional integration

- Integrate risk management with design controls, document control, CAPA, management review, and internal audit.
- Coordinate risk documentation with FDA submissions, EU MDR technical documentation, ISO 13485 certification, and post-market requirements.
- Integrate clinical risk assessment, clinical investigation, post-market surveillance, and clinical evaluation updates.
- Provide training for document authors, system users, regulatory documentation, and ongoing support.

## Standards and best practices

- Follow ISO 13485, IEC 62304, ISO 14971, and applicable ICH guidelines.
- Use plain language, visual communication, modular documentation, and accessible formats.

## Pitfalls

- Do not treat risk management as a one-time activity.
- Do not omit foreseeable misuse, use errors, software, cybersecurity, or combination-product risks.
- Do not implement risk controls without effectiveness verification.
- Do not lose traceability between hazards, controls, and verification evidence.
- Do not allow uncontrolled document versions or distribution.
- Do not omit jurisdiction-specific documentation requirements.
- Do not use electronic records without validation, access control, and audit trails.

## Checklist

- Document control procedures are defined, approved, and followed.
- Document types, numbering, versions, and statuses are tracked.
- Review and approval roles are assigned.
- Change control records are complete.
- The document management system is validated where required.
- Electronic record controls meet 21 CFR Part 11 where applicable.
- Retention and disposal rules are defined.
- The risk management plan defines scope, criteria, and responsibilities.
- Hazard analysis covers intended use, misuse, hardware, software, cybersecurity, and human factors.
- Risk estimation and evaluation are documented with criteria and a risk matrix.
- Risk controls follow the hierarchy and are verified.
- Residual risk and benefit-risk decisions are documented.
- The risk management file is current and traceable.
- Documentation, risk, CAPA, audit, and submission records are cross-referenced.
- Quality metrics and audit findings are reviewed for improvement.
