# Copywriting
Write marketing copy that is clear, benefits‑driven, and prompts the target action.

## When to use
- You need fresh copy for a web page (homepage, landing, pricing, feature, about, product). 
- You want to rewrite or improve existing copy. 
- The request mentions "write copy for", "improve this copy", "headline help", or "CTA copy".

---
## 1. Gather Context
Ask the user for any missing details before you start.

| Category | Questions |
|----------|-----------|
| **Page purpose** | What type of page is this? What is the primary action you want visitors to take? Is there a secondary action? |
| **Audience** | Who is the ideal customer? What problem are they trying to solve? What have they already tried? What objections might they have? Which words do they use to describe the problem? |
| **Product / Offer** | What are you selling? How is it different from alternatives? What transformation or outcome does it deliver? What proof points (numbers, testimonials, case studies) exist? |
| **Context** | Where does traffic come from (ads, organic, email)? What does the visitor already know? What messaging have they seen before arriving? |

---
## 2. Core Copywriting Principles
1. **Clarity over cleverness** – every sentence must be instantly understandable.
2. **Benefits over features** – link each feature to a concrete outcome for the reader.
3. **Specificity over vagueness** – replace "save time" with "cut weekly reporting from 4 hours to 15 minutes".
4. **Customer language over company language** – mirror the words your audience uses in reviews, support tickets, or interviews.
5. **One idea per section** – each block should advance a single argument and flow logically to the next.

---
## 3. Writing Style Rules
- Use simple words ("use" not "utilize").
- Write active voice ("We generate reports" not "Reports are generated").
- Remove hedging words ("almost", "very", "really").
- Show outcomes, don’t just tell (e.g., "Generate more leads by seeing which companies visit your site").
- Never fabricate statistics or testimonials.

**Quick quality scan** before finalizing:
- No jargon that confuses the audience.
- No sentence with more than three conjunctions.
- No passive voice.
- No exclamation points.
- No empty buzzwords ("innovative", "streamline") without concrete meaning.

---
## 4. Page Structure Framework
### Above the Fold (First screen)
- **Headline** – single most important message, specific, conveys core value.
- **Sub‑headline** – expands headline, adds a second benefit or clarifies scope (1‑2 sentences).
- **Primary CTA** – action‑oriented button text that tells the reader what they get (e.g., "Start Free Trial", "Get My Report").
- **Supporting visual** – screenshot, demo, or hero image that reinforces the message.

### Common Headline Formulas
- *{Achieve desirable outcome} without {pain point}*
- *The {opposite of usual process} way to {outcome}*
- *Never {unpleasant event} again*
- *{Product type} for {target audience}*
- *You don’t have to {skill} to {outcome}*
- *{Achieve outcome} by {how product helps}*
- *{Question highlighting pain}*
- *Turn {input} into {outcome}*
- *(Optional) {Achieve outcome} in {timeframe}*

### Body Sections (choose 3‑5 that fit the page)
1. **Social proof bar** – logos, metric, or short testimonial.
2. **Problem / Pain** – articulate the reader’s problem better than they can; paint the "before" picture.
3. **Solution / Benefits** – 3‑5 benefit blocks. Each block: headline, 1‑2 sentence benefit, optional proof point.
4. **How it works** – 3‑4 numbered steps, each showing a simple action and the resulting outcome.
5. **Detailed social proof** – testimonial with name, role, company, and specific result.
6. **Objection handling** – FAQ, comparison table, or guarantee.
7. **Final CTA** – restate value, repeat primary CTA, add genuine urgency or risk reversal.

### CTA Copy Guidelines
- **Weak**: "Submit", "Learn More", "Click Here".
- **Strong**: "Start Free Trial", "Get My Checklist", "See the Demo", "Book My Demo", "Download the Guide".
- **Formula**: *[Action verb] + [What they get] + (optional qualifier)*.

---
## 5. Editing Passes (Seven‑Sweep Framework)
After drafting, run these focused sweeps. Each sweep should be completed before moving to the next.

1. **Clarity** – Highlight confusing sentences, unclear pronouns, jargon, or missing context. Recommend specific rewrites.
2. **Voice & Tone** – Ensure a consistent brand personality (casual, professional, playful, etc.). Smooth any tonal shifts.
3. **So What?** – For every claim, ask "so what?". Add a benefit bridge if missing.
4. **Prove It** – Verify every claim has evidence (testimonials, stats, case studies, guarantees). Add or soften unsupported statements.
5. **Specificity** – Replace vague language with numbers, timeframes, or concrete examples.
6. **Heightened Emotion** – Add sensory details, micro‑stories, or rhetorical questions to make the copy feel.
7. **Zero Risk** – Near each CTA, address objections, add trust signals, and state risk reversals (money‑back guarantee, free trial, no credit‑card required).

After the final sweep, run a quick pass for grammar, typos, and formatting.

---
## 6. Checklist
- [ ] Page purpose and primary CTA defined.
- [ ] Audience problem and language captured.
- [ ] Core benefit statement present in headline.
- [ ] Every feature linked to a benefit.
- [ ] At least one specific proof point (number, testimonial, case study).
- [ ] Headline follows a proven formula.
- [ ] Sub‑headline adds specificity.
- [ ] Primary CTA uses strong verb + concrete outcome.
- [ ] Sections follow logical flow (problem → solution → proof → CTA).
- [ ] Language is simple, active, and confident.
- [ ] No vague buzzwords; all claims are specific.
- [ ] Emotional triggers (pain, desire, relief) are present.
- [ ] Risk reversals and trust signals near CTA.
- [ ] Final proofread for spelling, grammar, and link functionality.

---
## 7. Output Format
When you deliver copy, structure it with clear labels:
```
**Headline:** …
**Subheadline:** …
**Primary CTA:** …
**Section 1 – Problem:** …
**Section 2 – Benefits:** …
**Section 3 – How It Works:** …
**Section 4 – Social Proof:** …
**Section 5 – FAQ / Objections:** …
**Final CTA:** …
```
For each headline and CTA, optionally provide 2‑3 alternatives with a brief rationale.
